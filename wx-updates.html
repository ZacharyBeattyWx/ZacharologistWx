For the next 0–7 days, the Carolinas and Virginia look locked into a generally warm, increasingly humid pattern with temperatures running above normal most days. High pressure over the Southeast should keep the region largely summerlike, though periodic disturbances moving in from the west will bring rounds of scattered afternoon and evening thunderstorms. Especially across the mountains/Piedmont and then drifting eastward. Severe weather risk appears localized rather than widespread, but any stronger storms could produce heavy rain, gusty winds, and frequent lightning.

During the 8–14 day period, the broader pattern still favors a continued warm-to-hot regime across NC, SC, and VA, with above-normal temperatures remaining the dominant signal. The Southeast ridge looks persistent, meaning humidity levels likely stay elevated and the region trends toward a more typical early-summer setup with warm mornings, muggy afternoons, and intermittent thunderstorm chances rather than prolonged cool or dry periods. Rainfall overall may average near to slightly above normal because of repeated moisture flow from the Gulf and Atlantic, but no strong signal currently points toward a sustained washout pattern.
